Sons of the Silverstream

A Westeros Story

Fan fiction set during the War of the Five Kings

When Robert Baratheon dies and the realm fractures, the great lords call their banners—and the cost falls on the smallfolk. At Stony Mill, a forgotten holding on the Silverstream, two brothers receive a summons they cannot refuse. Corin is seventeen. Tommer is twenty-four. Neither has held a spear. This is the story of the men who fill the back ranks: the ones who march because a knight wrote their names in a book, who fight in wars they didn't start, and who carry nothing but mended boots, a father's advice, and each other.

        About the Story

        The World of Westeros

        The Story

        Sons of the Silverstream is a trilogy set in the world of Westeros during the War of the Five Kings. It follows Corin and Tommer, two brothers from a forgotten mill on the Silverstream, as they are swept into the great conflicts of their age. This is not a story of kings or queens, but of the smallfolk who bear the true cost of war.

        The World

        The kingdom of the Seven Realms is vast, and most of its people live and die far from the throne room. They farm, they trade, they build mills and keep the realm alive. When war comes, these ordinary people are called upon to march. Corin and Tommer's story happens in the margins of the great historical eventsânot in the war councils of lords, but on muddy roads, in river crossings, and in the tents of levied soldiers who never asked for this fight.

        The Themes

        This trilogy explores duty, brotherhood, the cost of ambition, and what it means to survive when the world around you is burning. It asks what people owe to each other, to their families, and to the uncertain future. Above all, it's about two brothers trying to keep each other alive in a world that wasn't kind to them to begin with.

        Why I Wrote This

        George R.R. Martin created a magnificent world full of intricate politics and complex characters. But for every person in the throne room, a thousand ordinary people are living ordinary lives. I wanted to tell that storyânot from the perspective of lords and kings, but from the viewpoint of the people who march when summoned, who fight in armies, who watch their homes burn. That's where the real story is.

        I hope you find yourself in these pages, in the struggles of Corin and Tommer, in the bonds between brothers, and in the quiet moments of humanity that persist even in the darkest times.
